A learning community runs on more than learners. Employers meet candidates with evidence; mentors and hosts give the hours their patience is worth; donors fund instruction they can audit.

For employers

The program is built so learners finish with artifacts, not just certificates: checked workflows, documented skills with test cases, and one truthful case study. The standard behind those artifacts is public:

“Explain it in your own words. Do it on a fresh example. Handle one variation. Privately, untimed, repeated until solid — a miss means we re-teach, not that you failed.”

Mentors and accessibility practitioners

Patient practitioners — developers, analysts, testers, technical writers, accessibility specialists — can host an hour, review learner work, or pair on the harder repeats. Roles, screening, supervision, data access, and time commitments are defined in writing before anyone participates.

Offer an hour: use the contact form’s “Volunteer or mentor” topic and name the skill you’d bring.
