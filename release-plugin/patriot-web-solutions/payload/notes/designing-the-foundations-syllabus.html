Six weeks, one artifact per week, and a readiness standard instead of a grade. Here is why the foundations syllabus is shaped the way it is.

Artifacts over quizzes. Each week ends with something the learner keeps: a task attempted with and without AI, a personal red-lines checklist, a reusable brief, a source-check worksheet, a documented workflow, a private demonstration record. A quiz measures a moment; an artifact is evidence the learner can reuse and an employer can respect.

Week one is calibration. The first artifact pairs the same task done with and without AI, with notes. Before anyone builds anything, they need a felt sense of where the tool helps, where it fails, and where it lies confidently.

Privacy is week two on purpose. Learners write their personal red lines before the course asks them to paste anything real into any system. The order is the lesson.

The standard, verbatim: “Explain it in your own words. Do it on a fresh example. Handle one variation. Privately, untimed, repeated until solid — a NO-GO means we retrain, not that you failed.” Train to standard, not to time: the calendar bends, not the standard.

This syllabus is a draft in the exact sense: the program owner reviews it before enrollment opens, and this note records the design reasoning so the review has something to argue with.
